# Technical Prose Rules

Based on *The Elements of Agent Style* by Yizhou Zhao, licensed under [CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/). The source is https://github.com/yzhao062/agent-style.

Use these rules as judgment aids. Preserve a sentence when the rule would make it less accurate, less clear, or less natural.

## Reader and Substance

- **RULE-01, critical:** Name the reader. Define terms that the reader cannot reasonably know, and explain purpose before mechanics.
- **RULE-02, high:** Prefer active voice when the actor matters. Passive voice is valid when the actor is unknown or irrelevant.
- **RULE-03, critical:** Replace vague or general claims with concrete mechanisms, names, dates, measurements, or boundaries already supported by the source.
- **RULE-04, high:** Remove words and phrases that add no meaning.
- **RULE-05, high:** Remove stock metaphors and prefabricated phrases.
- **RULE-06, high:** Replace avoidable jargon with common words. Preserve necessary domain terms and define them once.
- **RULE-07, medium:** State affirmative claims directly. Avoid reflexive `X, not Y` and `not only X but Y` framing.
- **RULE-08, high:** Match claim strength to evidence. Distinguish observed, measured, suggested, and proved results.

## Sentence and Document Structure

- **RULE-09, medium:** Keep parallel ideas in parallel grammatical form.
- **RULE-10, medium:** Keep subjects, verbs, modifiers, and referents close enough to parse once.
- **RULE-11, medium:** Put new or important information near the end of a sentence when this improves emphasis.
- **RULE-12, medium:** Split sentences longer than 30 words unless their structure remains clearer intact.
- **RULE-A, high:** Use bullets for real lists, not for a causal argument or a sentence broken into fragments.
- **RULE-B, medium:** Avoid casual em and en dashes in technical prose. Preserve numeric ranges and names.
- **RULE-C, low:** Vary repeated sentence openings when repetition has no deliberate purpose.
- **RULE-D, medium:** Remove repeated transition openers such as `Additionally`, `Furthermore`, and `Moreover`.
- **RULE-E, medium:** Do not close each paragraph by restating it.
- **RULE-F, high:** Use one term for one concept. Do not rotate synonyms or redefine abbreviations.
- **RULE-G, project:** Follow the existing heading-capitalization convention. Do not impose title case on a sentence-case project.
- **RULE-H, critical:** Support factual claims with a named source, concrete evidence, or a verifiable project artifact. Flag missing support; never invent it.
- **RULE-I, low:** In formal technical prose, use full forms instead of contractions unless the project voice permits them.

## Review Order

1. Resolve critical reader and evidence faults.
2. Resolve high-severity clarity and terminology faults.
3. Resolve structural and mechanical faults.
4. Apply low-severity polish only when it fits the project's voice.
